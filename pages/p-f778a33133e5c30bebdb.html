格挡是使用任意武器或盾格挡时，降低受到生物攻击伤害的机制。

格挡

玩家格挡时：

  1. 原始伤害先只按格挡物品，即盾或武器的护甲及抗性减免，参见详细公式。
  2. 最多消耗10点耐力，与剩余伤害占原始伤害的比例成正比。
  3. 以下情况下格挡可能失败：
    1. 剩余伤害填入失衡条，上限为玩家最大生命的40%，达到时玩家也会踉跄。
    2. 耐力不足，见第二步；此时玩家不会踉跄。
  4. 成功时，剩余伤害再按普通护甲及抗性减免。失败时，未经过格挡减伤的原始伤害直接按装备护甲及抗性计算。

补充说明：

  • 仅物理劈砍、穿刺、钝击及闪电伤害填充踉跄条。火焰、冰霜、毒素及灵魂也受格挡护甲减免，但不使玩家踉跄。
  • 格挡技能每点技能提高格挡护甲0.5%，等级100达到50%。
  • 技能提供的格挡护甲不舍入，物品提示除外。
  • 格挡技能通过提高格挡护甲，间接降低耐力消耗。
  • 格挡根据使用的格挡护甲减少收到的击退。
  • 格挡力是挡下近战攻击时对攻击者造成的击退量，按实际使用的格挡护甲，从50%缩放至100%。
  • 成功格挡会防止毒素等状态施加。
  • 邪骨恶灵之力激活时，格挡不耗耐力，反而返还5点。

实例

实例A

玩家A最大生命80，持1级青铜圆盾，格挡护甲16，格挡技能20，无抗性。

玩家A受40点攻击。先检查格挡护甲是否大于伤害一半，或不大于。圆盾基础格挡16，原文按每技能等级0.05%增加，20级为10%，计算如下：

格挡16 + (20 x 0.05%)与40/2比较，即格挡17.6与20点伤害比较。

伤害超过格挡护甲两倍，按基础公式，实际伤害等于伤害减护甲，即40 – 17.6 = 22.4。

剩余比例22.4 / 40 = 56%，因此消耗10点耐力的56%，即5.6点。

再检查剩余伤害是否造成踉跄。玩家A最大生命80，若剩余物理伤害22.4达到上限，即80点生命的40%，则踉跄、格挡失败，承受完整伤害。幸好80的40%为32，大于22.4，因此不踉跄，格挡成功。

剩余22.4点伤害再与玩家A装备护甲比较，计算最终伤害。

实例B

玩家B最大生命100，持1级木盾，格挡护甲6，格挡技能20，无抗性。

玩家B受50点攻击。先检查格挡值是否大于伤害一半，或不大于：

格挡6 + (20 x 0.05%)与50/2比较，即格挡6.6与25点伤害比较。

与玩家A一样，伤害超过格挡护甲两倍，因此伤害减护甲，即50 – 6.6 = 43.4。

剩余比例43.4 / 50 = 86.6%，因此消耗10点耐力的86.6%，即8.66点。

再次检查踉跄。玩家B上限为40，即最大生命100的40%，剩余伤害43.4，因此踉跄、格挡失败。不仅陷入踉跄，全部50点伤害绕过格挡，再按装备护甲计算最终伤害。

实例C

玩家C生命60，持3级青铜圆盾，格挡护甲28，格挡技能20，无抗性。

玩家C也受50点攻击。再次检查格挡值是否大于伤害一半，或不大于：

格挡28 + (20 x 0.05%)与50/2比较，即格挡32.8与25点伤害比较。

此时格挡大于伤害一半，计算不同：伤害平方除以四倍护甲，即2,500（50乘50）除以131.2（32.8乘4），结果玩家C受19.05点伤害。

剩余比例19.05 / 50 = 38.1%，因此消耗10点耐力的38.1%，即3.81点。

再比较玩家C踉跄上限。伤害19.05，上限24，即最大生命60的40%。低于上限，格挡成功，再按装备护甲计算剩余19.05点伤害的最终值。

弹反

攻击命中前不足0.25秒进行格挡，会触发招架，表现为红色溅射与明显金属碰撞声，通常令敌人踉跄。

弹反遵循格挡的规则，但有以下例外：

  • 格挡物品护甲乘以武器或盾牌的招架加成，见下表。
  • 弹反不消耗耐力。
  • 弹反成功时，攻击者会 失衡 （踉跄期间无法移动，并受到双倍伤害）。
    • 首领等部分生物免疫踉跄。
  • 塔盾等部分物品不能招架。

最大可格挡伤害

假定踉跄条为空，可根据两个主要因素计算不踉跄时最大格挡或招架伤害：

  • 玩家最大生命值的40%。
  • 格挡护甲：物品提示的黄色括号数值。招架时再乘以招架倍率。

最大可格挡伤害分两种情况：

  1. 若玩家最大生命的40%大于格挡护甲乘招架加成，则最大可格挡伤害为两者相加：MaximumBlockedDamage=0.4×MaximumLife+BlockArmor×ParryBonus
  2. 若玩家最大生命的40%小于格挡护甲乘招架加成，则最大可格挡伤害按公式：MaximumBlockedDamage=2×0.4×MaximumLife×BlockArmor×ParryBonus

此时公式更复杂，但情况1的公式仍是不错的近似。

说明

  • 招架除使敌人踉跄外，还比持续格挡能抵御更多伤害，但时机失误可能让攻击穿过。
  • 佑护之杖的护盾可招架，无需担心踉跄。

Blocking is a mechanic that reduces the damage received from creature attacks while blocking with any weapons or shields.

Blocking

When the player is blocking:

  1. Raw damage is reduced a first time by using only the blocking item's (shield or weapon) armor and resistances (cf. detailed formula).
  2. Up to 10 stamina is consumed, proportionally to ratio between leftover damage and raw damage.
  3. Blocking may fail if:
    1. The Stagger bar (40% of player max health) is filled with the leftover damage, in this case the player is also staggered.
    2. There is not enough stamina available (see step two), in this case the player is not staggered.
  4. If successful, the leftover damage is reduced a second time by using the regular armor value and resistances. If block failed, the raw damage without blocking reduction is applied to the regular armor value and resistances.

Additional details:

  • Only physical Slash, Pierce, Blunt and Lightning damage contribute to fill the stagger bar. Fire, Frost, Poison and Spirit damage are affected by the block armor, but do not contribute to stagger the player.
  • Blocking skill increases Block armor by 0.5% for each skill point, reaching 50% at level 100.
  • Block armor from the skill is not rounded (except on the tooltip).
  • Blocking skill indirectly reduces the Stamina usage by increasing the Block armor.
  • Blocking reduces the amount of received knockback based on the used Block armor.
  • Block force is the amount of knockback dealt to the attacker when blocking melee attacks. It scales from 50% to 100% based on the used Block armor.
  • Successful block prevents the application of statuses, like poison, etc.
  • When Bonemass Power is active, blocks no longer consume stamina and instead give back 5 stamina.

Examples

Example A

Player A has a maximum health of 80, is carrying a tier 1 bronze buckler (block armor 16), and have a blocking skill of 20 and no resistances.

Player A takes a hit for 40 damage. The first step of damage versus Block determines if the block armor value is greater than half the damage, or equal or less. The player has a base block with the buckler of 16, and the blocking skill increases it by 0.05% per level. At level 20, that would be 10%. This is calculated as:

Block 16 + (20 x 0.05%) compared to 40/2, or Block 17.6 compared to 20 damage

The damage is more than double the block armor level, so according to base damage formula, the damage taken equals damage minus armor. 40 – 17.6 would be 22.4.

The damage reduced correspond to 22.4 / 40 = 56% of the initial damage, so the player loses 56% of 10 = 5.6 stamina.

The next step is to calculate if the remaining damage causes a stagger effect. Player A has a maximum 80 health. If the remaining physical damage (22.4) was equal to or greater than the player's Stagger limit (40% of 80 health), they would be Staggered, the Block would fail, and the full amount of the damage would be applied to the character. Luckily for player A, 40% of 80 health is 32, and since that is more than the remaining 22.4 damage, they are not Staggered and the Block succeeds.

The remaining 22.4 damage would then be compared to player A’s armor worn to calculate the full damage that the player takes.

Example B

Player B has a maximum 100 health, is carrying a tier 1 wooden shield (block armor 6), and have a blocking skill of 20 and no resistances.

Player B takes a hit for 50 damage. First, the check is made to see if the Block value is greater, or equal or less than, half the damage taken, calculated as:

Block 6 + (20 x 0.05%) compared to 50/2, or Block 6.6 compared to 25 damage

Just as with player A, the damage is more than double the Block armor value, so damage taken equals damage minus armor. This would be 50 – 6.6, or 43.4.

The damage reduced correspond to 43.4 / 50 = 86.6% of the initial damage, so the player loses 86.6% of 10 = 8.66 stamina.

Again, the system will calculate if the remaining damage causes a Stagger effect. In this case, the Stagger limit for player B is 40 (40% of 100 max health), and the damage taken is 43.4, so player B is Staggered and the Block fails. This means that not only is player B left with a Staggered status, but all of the 50 damage bypasses the Block and is compared to the player’s worn armor to determine final damage taken.

Example C

Player C has 60 health, is carrying a tier 3 bronze buckler (block armor 28), and have a blocking skill of 20 and no resistances.

Player C also takes a hit for 50 damage. Again, a check is made to see if the Block value is greater, or equal or less than, half the damage taken, calculated as:

Block 28 + (20 x 0.05%) compared to 50/2, or Block 32.8 compared to 25 damage

In this case, the Block value is greater than half the damage, so the calculation of damage taken is a little different. Because the Block armor is greater than half the damage, the calculation is (damage squared) divided by (four times armor value), or 2,500 (50 times 50) divided by 131.2 (32.8 times 4). The result is that player C would take 19.05 damage.

The damage reduced correspond to 19.05 / 50 = 38.1% of the initial damage, so the player loses 38.1% of 10 = 3.81 stamina.

This is again checked against player C’s Stagger limit. Damage is 19.05, and the Stagger limit is 24 (40% of 60 max health). The damage is below the Stagger limit, so the Block is successful and the remaining 19.05 damage is compared to player C’s worn armor to calculate actual damage taken.

Parrying

Parrying happens when a block action is performed shortly (less than 0.25 seconds) before an attack lands, indicated by a red splash effect and a notable clanging sound, generally followed by an enemy being staggered.

Parrying follows the same ruleset as blocking except:

  • The blocking item armor is multiplied by the Parry Bonus of the weapon or shield (see table below).
  • Parrying does not cost any stamina.
  • If the parry succeeds, the attacker will Stagger (will be unable to move and will take double damage for the duration of the stagger).
    • Some creatures, like bosses, are immune to stagger.
  • Parrying is not available as an action to some items, like Tower Shields.

Maximum blocked damage

The maximum damage a player can block or parry without being staggered, assuming an empty stagger bar, can be calculated using two main components:

  • 40% of the player's Maximum Life.
  • Block Armor: This value can be found on the item tooltip, shown in yellow parentheses. For parrying, this value is to be multiplied by the parry multiplier.

To determine the maximum blocked damage, there are two cases:

  1. If 40% of the player's Maximum Life is greater than the Block Armor multiplied by the Parry Bonus, then the maximum blocked damage is the sum of the two values:
    MaximumBlockedDamage=0.4×MaximumLife+BlockArmor×ParryBonus
  2. If 40% of the player's Maximum Life is less than the Block Armor multiplied by the Parry Bonus, then the maximum blocked damage is given by the formula:
    MaximumBlockedDamage=2×0.4×MaximumLife×BlockArmor×ParryBonus

In this case, the formula is more complex, but the formula from case 1 still provides a good approximation.

Notes

  • Parrying allows the player to block more damage than the static block in addition to staggering enemies - but mistiming could let an attack through.
  • The Staff of Protection's shield offers the ability to parry without worrying about stagger.
