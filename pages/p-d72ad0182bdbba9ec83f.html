矮人是主要生活在迷雾之地、既可能被动也可能敌对的生物。矮人是智慧种族，体形矮小、皮肤蓝色，眼睛发出蓝至紫色光，有白色长须和辫子。灰烬之地中的矮人是红发，属于与迷雾之地矮人不同的氏族。

传记

矮人很特殊：未被攻击时对玩家保持被动。它们兼具创造力和魔法能力，能够建造精巧建筑。

在玩家到来前的某个未知时期，矮人封印了蛊虫之后，并将开启腐疫地堡的钥匙碎片散布在世界各处。

关系其他种族

行为

所有矮人，即矮人刺客、矮人法师、灰烬之地矮人和被囚禁的矮人，都属于矮人阵营，默认不与玩家敌对。玩家伤害矮人时，20米内所有可被激怒的生物都会敌对；玩家在其守护石范围内损伤物体超过五次，或摧毁一个物体时，矮人也会敌对。被激怒后会持续敌对。未被激怒的矮人不会攻击驯养生物生物。[ 1 ]

对其他生物，矮人遵循多数阵营的规则：除野兔和首领外，与所有其他阵营互相敌对，包括灰矮人、巨魔、丑地精、邪蛛探索者、亡灵、灰烬亡灵以及极北之地中的巨人战士和巨人巫师。[ 2 ]

游戏中文本

游戏文本将矮人描写为封闭且带有种族偏见的族群：未被挑衅时自顾自生活，但会谴责整个邪蛛探索者物种，被激怒后还会对人类使用种族辱骂。迷雾之地的一块符文石写道：

“矮人是远古伟大工匠的后裔，挖掘大地深处，寻找隐藏宝藏。

在英灵神殿，他们开采巨人的骨骼，提炼魔法物质埃达之能。只要不被打扰，他们便乐于执行自己的计划，少管他人生活；但被激怒时，会奋起自卫。"

[3]

福金说：“嘎——嘎！倔强的蠢货！矮人能容忍陌生人与流浪者，但惹恼他们就会迅速发怒……我相信你迟早会做到。”[ 4 ]雾尼则称他们为“失落的矮人氏族，早已与尼达维勒的同族分离”。[ 5 ]

乌尔夫留下的一块迷雾之地符文石也有种族偏见，将整个矮人族刻画为不懂玩笑的“小个子”，声称侮辱他们后只能将其杀死：“听从乌尔夫的话，别从小个子头上拿走帽子。他们不懂玩笑，侮辱他们后唯一的解决办法就是杀死他们。而且，他们的帽子不适合普通人的头。”[ 6 ]

人类

矮人称玩家为“北欧人”或“人类”。多数台词只是好奇：“所有人类都长你这样吗？你真是……算了。”[ 7 ]以及“当人类一定很奇怪吧。想象一下，还得钻过狭小空间！”[ 8 ]玩家碰他们的财物时，会警告：“我们先来的！”[ 9 ]和“你是好客人，我们才会是好主人。”[ 10 ]

对人类的辱骂同样针对种族，而非行为，但只在矮人被激怒后出现：“杀死那个北欧人！”[ 11 ]和“人类渣滓！”[ 12 ][ 13 ]

霍尔德说：“我以前见过你们这种人。”[ 14 ]

邪蛛探索者

矮人对邪蛛探索者的敌意也带有种族偏见。他们谴责整个物种，而非个别邪蛛，希望它们“全都萎缩死掉”，并称占领旧矿洞的它们为“害虫”：

  • “该死的邪蛛！真希望它们全都萎缩死掉。”[ 15 ]
  • “该死的邪蛛砸坏了我们的黑暗之核！看来又得回到深处了……”[ 16 ]
  • “那些邪蛛可不好惹……”[ 17 ]
  • “这些害虫蔓延得比我担心的更远。照这样下去，我们所有旧矿洞都会被占领。”[ 18 ]

巨人

玩家离开时，被囚禁的矮人可能说：“尽可能多杀些巨人族！”[ 19 ]

灰烬亡灵

灰烬之地矮人将灰烬亡灵作为整体谈论：“那些亡灵混蛋可真囤了不少漂亮宝石……”[ 20 ]以及“昨天我们可把那些焦骨打惨了，对吧？”[ 21 ]

宗族之间

灰烬之地矮人自称红胡子，并对另一氏族白胡子夸耀：

  • “哈！白胡子绝不敢来这里冒险。”[ 22 ]
  • “白胡子绝对受不了这热度。他们根本没这个胆量！”[ 23 ]
  • “我可无法在同一个挖掘点驻扎几个月。我们红胡子生来就是要探索的！”[ 24 ]
  • “白胡子听说我们的成功，一定会嫉妒！”[ 25 ]

位置

多数矮人只在迷雾之地出现，常住在用大理石、铜和木材建造的建筑内。许多建筑和营地受守护石保护；损伤建筑、内部物品或周围矮人，会让附近所有矮人敌对。除初始生成者外，还可在整个生物群系中以1–2人的小队生成。

商人霍尔德和希尔德各在每个世界出现一次，分别位于黑暗林山和青青草原。无法激怒或伤害，也不会离开固定地点。他们用金币收购贵重物品。霍尔德的库存随献祭更多弃誓者而增加，希尔德的库存则随找回并归还她的箱子而增加。

灰烬之地废墟中也能遇到一些幸存矮人。

说明

  • “dvergr”[ 26 ]是古诺尔斯语中北欧神话侏儒的称呼，复数为“dvergar”。
  • 矮人是目前英灵神殿中唯一既可能对玩家被动、也可能敌对的种族。
  • Iron Gate博客的介绍称霍尔德是“dvergr”，即矮人[27]，他在众多台词之一中也这样称呼自己。
    • 他还说自己即使在矮人中也算“矮”，用来解释身高。北欧神话的矮人[ 28 ]最初并未被描述为身材矮小。
    • 这也可能只是他的玩笑或双关，毕竟英灵神殿中的其他矮人身高同样较矮。

A Dvergr is a type of both passive and aggressive creature found primarily in the Mistlands. The Dvergr are an intelligent race that resemble short, blue humanoids with glowing eyes ranging from blue to purple, and long white beards and braids. The Dvergr living in the Ashlands, are red haired and of a different clan than those from the Mistlands.

Biography

Dvergr are unique creatures in that they are passive to the player unless attacked. They demonstrate both ingenuity and magical abilities, which allows them to construct elaborate structures.

At some unknown point in time prior to the player's arrival, the Dvergr sealed away The Queen and scattered the key fragments to unlock the Infested Citadel across the world.

Relations with other races

Behavior

All Dvergr (Dvergr Rogue, Dvergr Mage, Ashlands Dvergr and Imprisoned Dvergr) belong to the Dvergr faction. Players and the Dvergr faction are not enemies by default. A Dvergr only becomes hostile to players once it is aggravated: when a player deals damage to a Dvergr, every creature within 20 meters that can be aggravated turns hostile, and a Dvergr also turns hostile after a player damages objects inside its Ward's area more than five times, or destroys one. An aggravated Dvergr stays hostile. Tamed creatures are not attacked by a Dvergr unless that Dvergr is aggravated.[1]

Toward other creatures, the Dvergr follow the same rule as most creature factions: they are hostile to every other faction except Hares and bosses, and those factions are hostile to them in return. This includes Greydwarfs and Trolls, Fulings, Seekers, the undead, the Charred and the Krigen and Hexen of the Deep North.[2]

In-game text

The in-game text portrays the Dvergr as insular but racially motivated: they keep to themselves unless provoked, yet condemn the Seekers as a species and hurl racial slurs at humans once aggravated. A Runestone in the Mistlands reads:

"The Dvergr are the descendants of the great smiths of old, delvers in the deep earth, seekers of hidden treasure.

Here in Valheim they mine the bones of the Jotunn and distill eitr, the stuff of magic. If left alone, they are happy with their own plans and care little for the lives of others, but when raised they will defend themselves."

[3]

Hugin says: "Kra-kraaa! Stubborn fools! The Dvergr will tolerate strangers and vagrants but they are quick to anger should you upset them... Which I am sure you will manage to do sooner or later."[4] Munin calls them "the forlorn Dvergr clans, long since separated from their kin in Nidavellir".[5]

A Mistlands runestone left by Ulf is racially motivated: it stereotypes the Dvergr as a whole race as humorless "short ones" whose only cure for insult is killing: "Heed the words of Ulf and do not take the hats of the short ones from their heads. They have no sense of a jest and the only cure for insulting them is to kill them. Also, their hats do not fit common heads."[6]

Humans

The Dvergr address the player as "Norseman" or "human". Most of these lines are curious rather than hostile: "Do all humans look like you? You're so... Nevermind."[7] and "It must be strange, being human. Imagine having to crawl through small spaces!"[8] When a player touches their property, they warn: "We were here first!"[9] and "We're only good hosts as long as you're a good guest."[10]

Their slurs toward humans are likewise aimed at the player's race rather than the player's actions, though they are only said once a Dvergr has become aggravated: "Kill the Norseman!"[11] and "Human scum!"[12][13]

Haldor says "I've met your kind before."[14]

Seekers

The Dvergr's hostility toward the Seekers is racially motivated. Rather than objecting to individual Seekers, they condemn the species as a whole, wishing that they would "all just shrivel up and die" and calling them "pests" that have overrun their old mines:

  • "Blasted seekers! I wish they'd all just shrivel up and die."[15]
  • "Damned seekers smashed our black cores! Back into the deep then, I guess..."[16]
  • "Those seekers are not to be messed with..."[17]
  • "These pests have spread further than I feared. At this rate, all our old mines will be overtaken."[18]

Giants

An Imprisoned Dvergr may say "Slay all the giantkind you can!" as the player leaves.[19]

Charred

The Ashlands Dvergr speak of the Charred as a group: "Those undead bastards sure hoard some pretty gemstones..."[20] and "We sure hit those charred hard yesterday, didn't we?"[21]

Between clans

The Ashlands Dvergr call themselves Redbeards and boast about another clan, the Whitebeards:

  • "Ha! A Whitebeard would never dare to venture here."[22]
  • "The Whitebeards could never withstand this heat. They just don't have the stones for it!"[23]
  • "I could never remain stationed at just one excavation for months. Us Redbeards are meant to explore!"[24]
  • "The Whitebeards will be jealous when they hear of our success, I'm sure!"[25]

Location

Most Dvergr are only encountered in the Mistlands, where they can often be found inhabiting structures they have built out of marble, copper, and wood. Many of their structures and encampments are protected by Wards, and damaging these structures or their contents (including any Dvergr inside or around them) makes all Dvergr in the immediate vicinity aggressive. In addition to these initial spawns, Dvergr can spawn throughout the biome in groups of 1–2.

Merchants Haldor and Hildir can each be found once per world, in the Black Forest and Meadows respectively. They cannot be angered or damaged, nor can they each move from their set piece. They pay for valuables with coins. Haldor's stock expands as more Forsaken are sacrificed, Hildir's as her chests are retrieved and returned.

Some Dvergr can be encountered in Ashlands surviving in the ruins.

Notes

  • The word "dvergr"[26] is the Old Norse word for the mythological dwarf of Norse mythology. The Old Norse plural of "dvergr" is "dvergar".
  • Dvergar are the only current race in Valheim that are both passive and aggressive to the player.
  • Haldor is a "dvergr" or "dwarf", according to his introduction on Iron Gate's blog[27], which is how he refers to himself by one of his many quotes.
    • He is also "short" for a dwarf, explaining his stature. Dwarves of Norse Mythology[28] were not originally described as diminutive.
    • This could also just be a joke or a pun on his part, especially as the other Dvergar seen in Valheim are similarly short.
